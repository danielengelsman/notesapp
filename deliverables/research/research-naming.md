# Naming & Collision Check — "Ledgerlift" and Alternatives
Date: 2026-07-11. Product: client-side QuickBooks Desktop escape tool — permanent offline archive + migration packs. Themes: escape, ownership, proof, permanence ("your books, yours again").

Method note: web search + GitHub API + DNS resolution. Direct page fetches to arbitrary domains were blocked by the sandbox egress proxy (403 on CONNECT), so domain status is verified via search-engine indexing + DNS, not rendered pages. No registrar interaction, nothing purchased.

## 4. Final ranking

1. **Bookstead** — WINNER. Zero collisions across web, GitHub, app stores, and trademark-search snippets. Two syllables, easy to say/spell. The homestead metaphor nails three of four brand themes at once: ownership (your land, your deed), permanence (a stead endures), and escape's destination (you don't just flee QuickBooks — you settle somewhere you own). "Your books, safe at home." Domain parked, not in use — plausibly acquirable; .app/get- fallbacks exist. Clearest trademark runway of any candidate.
2. **Outledger** — Strong #2. Says the category and the escape in one word; totally unclaimed. Downsides: three syllables, and it lives in a phonetically mobbed "-ledger" namespace (Ledger, OpenLedger, Xledger, Overledger) where confusion and weak trademark distinctiveness are real risks.
3. **Permabook** — Clean and literal about permanence, but three syllables and says nothing about escape or ownership; feels more like a file format than a company.
4. Everbooks (minor collisions), 5. Ledgerport (clean, but -ledger crowding), 6. Freehold Books (great metaphor, weak mark), 7. Bookout (surname SEO), 8. Bookvault (eliminated — established company), 9. **Ledgerlift (eliminated — live identical USPTO filings + active same-field firm at ledgerlift.com)**.

**Recommendation: rename to Bookstead.** Before committing: run a formal USPTO TESS clearance (Cl. 9, 35, 42) with counsel and get a broker quote on bookstead.com — both consistent with everything found here, neither performable from this sandbox.

## 5. Tagline shortlist (anchored in hostage books / renewal ransom / ownership)

1. **"Your books, yours again."** — the positioning line itself; leads with restitution.
2. **"Stop renting your own numbers."** — names the absurdity of subscribing to see your own history.
3. **"No ransom. No renewal. Your books, on your disk."** — hits the renewal-ransom anger and the offline promise in one breath.
4. **"Twenty years of your books shouldn't need a login."** — the hostage-data pain, made concrete.
5. **"Walk out with everything."** — pure escape; pairs well with "migration packs."
